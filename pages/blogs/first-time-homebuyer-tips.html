First-Time Homebuyer Tips: Everything You Need to Know Before Buying a Houserrr

July 23, 2025
No Tags Assigned to this Post

Table Of Contents

When you buy your first home, you experience a range of emotions: the happiness of owning a house, along with the nervousness of making the right decision, and the challenge of financing it. To define, a first-time homebuyer is someone who has never owned a home (self or jointly). However, with many schemes introduced by the Indian government over the past years, buying a home has become easier than ever!

If you are considering purchasing a house and don’t know where to begin, keep reading. In this blog, you will find some useful first-time homebuyer tips. Before we get to it, however, let’s look at some advantages of buying over renting:

  • Equity vs Expense: Rent is an ongoing cost, while EMIs build equity.
  • Stability: Owning a home offers security, as opposed to the risk of tenancy being terminated by the landlord.
  • Customisation: You can renovate and modify your home freely.

Buyers also often have some common fears, like:

  • Long-term financial commitment
  • Fear of fraud or legal issues
  • A lack of understanding of loan terms and documentation.

Following a first-time homebuyer guide will also help you plan your finances better, which includes building and improving your credit score, saving for a down payment, and planning for additional costs, such as registration, taxes, and interiors.

Build Your Credit Score Before You Buy

Ideal Credit Score

Having a CIBIL score of 750 or more is considered perfect for getting a favourable home loan with good terms in India.

Here is how you can build your credit score:

  • Clear your credit card dues on time.
  • Maintaining a healthy credit utilisation ratio (<30%).
  • Avoid multiple loan applications in a short time.
  • Keep old credit accounts active.

What Not To Do Before Applying

  • Maintaining a credit score for first-time buyers is essential, so don’t take any new personal loans.
  • Avoid closing existing credit cards, as it will shorten your credit history.
  • Don’t delay your EMI or credit card payments.

Start Saving Early for a Down Payment

Down Payment Norms in India

  • Banks usually finance up to 75% to 90% of the property cost.
  • So, how much down payment do you need to buy a house? The down payment is usually 10% to 25% of the cost. (Usually higher for luxury or resale properties)

How to Set a Savings Goal

  • Saving for a house as a first-time buyer means that you need to estimate the property cost and loan eligibility.
  • Additionally, remember to plan to save at least Rs 5 lakhs to Rs 25 lakhs, depending on the city and property type.

Tools and Tips

  • Use a Recurring Deposit (RDs) and SIPs in mutual funds.
  • Use Public Provident Fund for long-term savings.
  • Automate savings transfers monthly.

Get Pre-Approved for a Mortgage First

Pre-Qualification vs Pre-Approval

  • Pre-Qualification: Informal estimate based on income.
  • Pre-Approval: Formal loan sanction after a document review.

What Indian Lenders Look For

  • You might be wondering how to get pre-approved for a mortgage. Indian lenders usually look for income stability: Are you salaried or self-employed?
  • They also verify your CIBIL score and existing debt.
  • FOIR (Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio)

Benefits of Pre-Approval

  • When you get pre-approved for a loan, it strengthens your negotiation power with developers.
  • Helps you shortlist properties within your range
  • The loan has a faster closing process.

First-Time Home Buyer Checklist: What You Will Need?

Here is a first-time home buyer checklist you can follow:

Essential Documents Needed to Buy a House

  • Identity and address proof (Aadhaar Card, PAN, Passport)
  • Income proof (Salary slips, ITR, Form 16)
  • Bank statements for the last 6 months.
  • Employment proof or business registration (for self-employed buyers)

Why Document Readiness Matters

Not submitting the documents needed to buy a house can lead to:

  • Loss of good home deals
  • Lender rejections
  • Increased processing time.

Tips:

  • Create a digital folder of scanned copies.
  • Ensure your KYC details are up to date.

Determine What You Really Need in a Home

Needs vs Wants

Determine your needs when it comes to your home, including the number of bedrooms, the distance to your workplace and other social infrastructure, and the basic amenities. Gyms, pools, modular kitchen, and a clubhouse will come under wants.

Other useful tips for buying your first home include considering the lifestyle factors, such as:

  • Do you want a house that will accommodate a work-from-home or hybrid job set up?
  • Your home should also be perfect for your family, and close to schools as well as hospitals.
  • Proximity to metro and retail shops is also essential.

Use a priority matrix to decide on must-haves vs good-to-haves.

Choose the Right Neighbourhood, Not Just the Right Home

Before you begin the hunt, understanding what to know before buying a house is important. This includes knowing the area you are primarily interested in. For example, if you are considering buying an apartment in L&T Realty Elixir Reserve – Powai, you will need to know about this project and the locality that surrounds it. Here are the key indicators you will need for the analysis.

  • Civic Infrastructure, such as roads, drainage, and garbage segregation, is in place.
  • Upcoming Developments like the metro and SEZs are being constructed.
  • The area is safe and has a good community reputation.

Research Tools

Make sure you do a thorough research before you choose the right location. Here are some useful tools and methods.

  • Google Maps and Street View
  • RERA portals
  • Visit the area at different times of the day.

Red Flags You Cannot Ignore

  • Legal issues with the land
  • Unfinished roads, sewage or water issues
  • Unusually low pricing

Understanding How Much Home You Can Afford

The 28/36 Rule (Used by Indian Banks)

Planning your finances when you are buying your first home can be confusing. If you are confused about budgeting to buy a home, then follow the 28/36 rule. According to this rule:

  • Maximum 28% of gross income should be spent on EMIs and housing-related costs.
  • Maximum 36% should go into repaying debts; this includes personal and car loans.

Other factors determine how much home you can afford:

  • Property Taxes
  • Maintenance charges (Rs 2 to Rs 6 per sq. ft per month)
  • Clubhouse and association fees

Use tools such as

  • Bank EMI calculators
  • Real estate affordability checkers.

Budget Beyond the Down Payment

Before you finalise on anything, consider your budget, especially:

Common Additional Costs:

  • Registration and Stamp Duty: 5% to 8% (varies by state)
  • GST (for under-construction properties): 1% (for affordable housing) or 5% (for others)
  • Brokerage: 1% to 2% of property value. (Only applicable for resale properties, if you are planning to buy through a broker.)
  • Legal and Loan Processing Fees: Rs 10,000 to Rs 50,000
  • Interior and Furnishing: Rs 1 lakhs to 10 lakhs depending on the home’s size.

For more clarity on the closing cost of your home, make a worksheet that includes:

  • Fixed costs (purchases and taxes)
  • Variable costs (moving and interiors)
  • Monthly budget post-EMI.

Explore First-Time Homebuyer Programs and Assistance

You can also consider the first-time homebuyer programs introduced by the Indian government. Some key schemes include:

  • PMAY (CLSS): Interest subsidy of upto Rs 2.67 lakh.
  • State Housing Boards: MHADA in Maharashtra, DDA in Delhi and Awas Yojana in Gujarat.
  • Tax Benefits:
    • Section 80C: Upto Rs 1.5 lakhs on principal payment.
    • Section 24: Upto Rs 2 lakhs on interest.
    • Section 80EEA: Additional Rs 1.5 lakhs for affordable homes.
  • You can also look for down payment assistance; the PMAY offers some help in this as well.

Eligibility

  • Family income limits, especially for PMAY.
  • No existing home in you or your spouse’s name.
  • Loan should be from a recognised lender.

Compare Mortgage Lenders Before You Commit

What to Compare

  • Interest rates
  • Processing fees
  • Prepayment penalties, although it is usually NIL for floating rate loans.

Banks vs NBFCs

  • When you compare mortgage lenders like banks and NBFCs, you will find that:
    • Banks offer better rates but have a strict criteria.
    • NBFCs, on the other hand, are more flexible, and offer better home loan eligibility for self-employed individuals.

Tools

  • RBI’s Bank Rate Index
  • Online home loan comparison portals like BankBazaar or PaisaBazaar.

Hire a Trusted Real Estate Agent Who Understands First-Time Buyer

When you are looking to hire a real estate agent to help make your first ever house purchase, you need to know how they can help you. Make sure the agent is RERA registered, has proper knowledge of the local area, and strong contacts with the developers.

Look out for Red Flags:

  • Pushy sales tactics
  • Avoiding questions about legal papers
  • Demanding high brokerage without transparency.

Here are some questions you can ask them:

  1. What is your commission?
  2. Can you assist with loan or legal help?
  3. Do you represent the buyer or seller?

Be Cautious When Buying Newly Constructed Homes

You have to be careful when buying new constructions, since there are often risks involved here:

  • Project Delays: One of the most common complaints in India, due to funding issues, labour shortage, or litigation.
  • Approval Issues: Builders may not have a complete land acquisition, RERA registration, or the necessary environmental clearances.
  • Hidden Costs: Extra charges for parking, GST, club membership and preferential location can inflate the final price.
  • Poor Quality or Finishing: Low-grade materials used in the final stages to cut costs.

Here are some real estate tips for first-time buyers:

  • Make sure the project you are considering is RERA-registered. You can visit your respective state’s RERA site for verification.
  • Check that the project has an occupancy certificate and completion certificate.
  • Ensure there is a written warranty on construction quality fittings.

Get A Professional Home Inspection Before You Close

Getting a home inspection is incredibly helpful. It usually covers:

  • Plumbing, electrical systems, seepage and roofing.
  • Ventilation, window alignment and tile or wood flooring issues.
  • Overall safety and compliance with building standards.

Inspectors often find some common problems, such as:

  • Cracks in walls, leaky ceilings, and poor drainage slopes in bathrooms.
  • Uneven flooring, loose fittings and exposed wiring
  • False ceiling moisture or termites in woodwork, especially in resale properties.

Get a home inspection and use the report to negotiate for a final price, or request repairs. You can also avoid expensive fixes after possession, since brand-new homes often have finishing flaws.

Here is a common home-inspection tip for buyers: Make sure to hire a local certified inspector or civil engineer. They will charge Rs 3000 to Rs 10,000, depending on the size and the city.

Buy a Homeowners Insurance Before Moving In

Why it is Essential in India

  • Buy homeowners insurance as it will protect against natural disasters: earthquakes, floods and fires.
  • They also cover cases of theft, burglary and vandalism.
  • Some insurance plans include coverage for furnishings and electronics.

Types of Coverage:

  • Basic Structure Insurance: For physical damage to the building
  • Content Insurance: For your belongings inside.
  • Comprehensive Plan: Both structure and contents

Common Exclusions

  • Wear and tear, war damage, pre-existing damage
  • Non-disclosure of modifications.

A useful first-time homebuyer advice you can follow is to add the following:

  • Loss of rent if you lease the property
  • Alternate accommodation benefit
  • Coverage for jewelry and gadgets. This will need a valuation proof.

Common Mistakes First-Time Homebuyers Make (And How to Avoid Them)

Rushing Into a Decision

  • Often, pressure from sellers or family can lead to first-time buyers making a wrong decision they might regret.
  • Visit multiple properties and don’t skip legal checks.

Overestimating Loan Eligibility

  • The pre-approved amount doesn’t mean affordability.
  • Remember to factor in EMIs and other debts.

Ignoring Resale Value

  • Pick a location with growing infrastructure and demand.
  • Avoid homes with non-Vastu layouts or poor floor plans.

Not Budgeting for Other Costs

  • This is another common mistake first-time homebuyers make; the stamp duty and registration costs as well as interiors are often missed in budgeting.
  • Use a full-cost calculator.

Skipping Legal Review

  • Always get an independent lawyer to verify titles, agreements and land use permissions.

Post-Purchase Financial Planning for First-Time Buyers

EMI Managements

  • Keeping EMI within 30% to 40% of monthly income.
  • Consider your SIP or RD for the annual property tax or society maintenance fund.

Build an Emergency Fund

  • Plan your EMIs and living costs for at least 6 months.
  • Use liquid mutual funds or high-interest savings accounts for your emergency fund

When to Refinance or Transfer Loan

  • Refinancing is essential if the interest rates fall by more than 1.5% from your rate.
  • After 12 EMIs, you can consider a balance transfer to another lender.

Home Buying Timeline: Start Saving Early and Plan Ahead

6 Months Before Purchase

  • Check your CIBIL score
  • Start budgeting and tracking expenses
  • Begin your RD or SIP for down payment fund
  • Research housing markets.

3 Months Before Purchase

  • Get pre-approved for a home loan.
  • Shortlist localities and visit projects.
  • Organise documents: income proof, KYC, bank statements

1 Month Before Purchase

  • Finalise property and conduct legal checks
  • Book home inspection
  • Budget for registration and moving
  • Arrange homeowner’s insurance

Conclusion: Your First-Time Homebuying Journey Starts with Knowledge

Your first-time home buying process starts with making informed decisions. Property purchase in India is complex; mistakes can be costly and irreversible. Being a prepared buyer gives you more power to negotiate, choose wisely and avoid fraud.

Use a personalised checklist, and talk to a real estate consultant. Use verified tools like RERA portals and home loans calculators.

Here is a recap of the checklist:

  • Save early
  • Build credit
  • Get pre-approved
  • Inspect the home
  • Know all costs
  • Buy insurance
  • Plan post-purchase finances

Frequently Asked Questions for First-Time Homebuyers in India

  1. What’s your best advice for first-time homebuyers?
    • Start early, build your credit score and don’t skip any legal and technical inspections.
  2. What is the 20/30/40 rule for buying a house?
    • The rule states that 20% of property cost goes as down payment. 30% of income for EMIs, and 40% is a savings cushion for emergencies and maintenance.
  3. What is the best option for first-time buyers?
    • Affordable housing projects under PMAY, or under construction projects with RERA approval often have a lower entry cost.
  4. What do most first-time home buyers get approved for?
    • 75% to 90% of the property’s value, although the decision is based on income and credit score.
  5. How much should I save for a down payment on my first home?
    • At least 10% to 25% of the total value, with an extra Rs 2 to 5 lakhs for closing costs.
  6. What documents do I need to buy a house as a first-time buyer?
    • You will need your PAN and Aadhaar card, salary slips or ITR, and last 6 months’ bank statements.
  7. What is the typical home buying process for first-time buyers?
    • It begins with research, pre-approval, property search, agreement, loan sanction, registration and possession.
  8. What is the difference between pre-qualification and pre-approval?
    • Pre-qualification is an informal estimate, while pre-approval is a formal loan offer based on verified documents.
  9. What are common mistakes first-time buyers make and how can I avoid them?
    • Rushed decisions, not budgeting for full costs and ignoring legal and resale aspects. You can avoid it by planning, researching and seeking help from experts.
  10. How do I check my eligibility for a home loan?
    • Use eligibility calculators available online. Banks like SBI, HDFC and ICICI often have it available on their website. Factors include age, income, liabilities and your CIBIL score.
